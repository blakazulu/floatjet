# Free VPN vs Paid VPN: What Remote Workers Need to Know (2025)

## The "Free" Trap

Okay, I get it. You're scrolling the app store and there's like fifty "FREE VPN" options. NordVPN wants $3/month and here's some random app promising the same thing for nothing. Why would anyone pay?

Here's the thing. I tried this route. Downloaded a few free VPNs back when I was being cheap. Thought I was smart.

I wasn't smart.

Running VPN servers costs money. Bandwidth costs money. If they're not charging you, they're making money some other way. Usually by selling your data. The exact thing a VPN is supposed to prevent.

---

## How Free VPNs Actually Stay Afloat

They're businesses, not charities. Here's what's really happening:

### They're Selling Your Data

Most common model. The VPN sees everythingevery site, every search, every login. They package that up and sell it to advertisers and data brokers.

A 2022 study found 75% of free VPN apps had tracking libraries baked in. That's not a typo. Three out of four.

### Ads Everywhere

Some inject ads into your browsing. Not just in the appinto websites you visit. I've seen this happen. Sketchy as hell.

### Your Internet Becomes Their Exit Node

This one's wild. Hola VPN got caught turning user devices into exit nodes for their network. Your IP address, other people's traffic running through it. One guy's internet connection was used for a botnet. Could've been yours.

### The Legit Model: Freemium

ProtonVPN and Windscribe do it right. Free tier with limits. Paid users subsidize free users. No shady stuff. This is the only model I trust.

### The Simple Question

How does this company make money? If you can't answer that clearly, assume it's your data.

---

## Security? What Security?

Beyond the privacy stuff, free VPNs are often actual security risks.

### Malware Built In

A CSIRO study: 38% of free Android VPN apps contained malware. These apps have deep access to your device. They see everything. Some capture passwords. Some install more malware.

### Garbage Encryption

Some use outdated protocols. Some implement encryption wrong. Either way, someone sophisticated could crack it.

Paid VPNs use real standardsAES-256, WireGuardand have actual security teams. Free VPNs? Maybe one developer who also does marketing.

### Leak City

I've tested free VPNs that leaked my real IP address. The whole point of a VPN, defeated. Your "protected" connection showing exactly who you are.

Paid VPNs include kill switches and leak protection. Standard features. Free VPNs? Usually don't.

### No Audits

NordVPN, ExpressVPN, Surfsharkall independently audited. Third parties checking if they actually do what they claim.

Free VPNs? Never seen one submit to an audit. Wonder why.

## The Numbers Don't Lie

**Speed:** Free VPNs throttle you. 5-20 Mbps when you could have 80-95 Mbps. Try doing a video call on that.

**Data:** Most cap you at 500MB-10GB per month. One Zoom meeting burns through a gig. One Netflix movie uses 3-7GB. You'd hit the limit before lunch.

**Servers:** Free VPNs offer maybe 3-10 locations. Paid ones give you 50-100+ countries. More options, less congestion, better speeds.

For actual remote work, free VPNs are basically unusable.

## When Free Might Be Okay

Look, I'm not saying never. There are situations:

**Quick one-off stuff:** Need to access one region-locked article? Don't need to log into anything sensitive? ProtonVPN's free tier is fine.

**Testing before buying:** Windscribe and ProtonVPN let you try the apps before committing. That's literally why free tiers exist.

**Rare public WiFi use:** You work from home 99% of the time? Only hit coffee shops occasionally? A free tier's data limit might work.

### When Free Is Never Okay

- Client data. Any client data.
- Banking or financial accounts
- NDAs, contracts, sensitive business stuff
- Daily remote work
- Streaming (doesn't work anyway)

If your work involves any actual responsibility, pay for the VPN.
