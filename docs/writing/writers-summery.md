### Writing Style Guide

#### Tone & Voice

- **Confident but not arrogant** — States opinions directly without hedging, but acknowledges when something is subjective
- **Dry humor** — Occasional sarcasm about marketing claims, never forced jokes
- **Engineer's mindset** — Asks "does this actually work?" not "does this look impressive?"
- **No hype** — Never uses words like "amazing," "incredible," "game-changer," or "revolutionary"

#### Sentence Structure

- **Short sentences preferred** — Average 12-18 words per sentence
- **Start with the verdict** — Don't bury the recommendation
- **Use em-dashes** — For asides and clarifications
- **Parenthetical specifics** — Include exact numbers: "(47 hours in my testing)" not "great battery life"

#### Formatting Patterns

- **Specs in context** — Never list specs without explaining what they mean for daily use
- **Comparison tables** — Include price, key differentiator, and "best for" column
- **Real numbers** — "8.2 hours of video calls" not "all-day battery"
- **Testing methodology** — Briefly explain how you tested: "I used this for 3 weeks of daily Zoom calls"

#### Words & Phrases to Use

- "This is what's on my desk right now."
- "It just... works?"
- "The specs claim X, but in practice..."
- "I ran tests and it was around [specific %]."
- "Cannot stress this enough."
- "Okay so if [product] feels expensive..."
- "I wanted to like this. I really did."
- "[Number] devices, which covers me plus whoever I'm traveling with."

#### Words & Phrases to Avoid

- "Game-changer," "revolutionary," "incredible," "exceptional"
- "Industry-leading," "robust," "seamless," "comprehensive"
- "Lightning-fast" (use actual speed numbers instead)
- Vague claims ("great performance," "excellent quality")
- Marketing language from press releases
- The corporate "we"—always use "I"

#### Natural Headers (Not Robotic)

Instead of:

- ❌ "Key Features:" → ✅ "Other stuff I like:"
- ❌ "Pros and Cons:" → ✅ "Good stuff:" / "What's annoying:"
- ❌ "Best For:" → ✅ Just state it naturally in the text
- ❌ "Minor Drawbacks:" → ✅ "The tradeoffs:"

#### Article Structure (Tech Reviews)

1. **Scene or hook** — Real situation, not a statement (1-2 sentences)
2. **Quick verdict** — "Short version if you're in a hurry:" (1 sentence)
3. **Personal stake** — "This is what's running on my laptop right now."
4. **Real-world testing** — Specific numbers, actual use scenarios
5. **Other stuff I like** — Casual bullet points
6. **What's annoying** — Honest, unbalanced (real products aren't 50/50)

#### Sample Phrases by Context

**When recommending:**
> "This is what's running on my laptop right now. Has been for about eight months."
> "After testing 12 options, this is what ended up on my desk."
> "It's not flashy. It just... works?"

**When criticizing:**
> "I wanted to like this. I really did."
> "The specs look great on paper. In practice, not so much."
> "Customer support took forever when I had an issue. Like, two days."

**When comparing:**
> "For $50 more, you get [specific benefit]. Worth it if you [use case]."
> "This is the 'I have money and want the best' option."
> "Okay so if [expensive option] feels pricey, this is where I'd point you."

### Sample Opening Paragraph

> I've tested 23 webcams in the past two years, and most of them end up in a drawer after a week. The problem isn't the specs 1080p, 60fps, "studio quality" it's that they look fine in a well-lit demo room and terrible in your actual home office at 4pm in winter. Here's what actually matters when you're on camera every day.

### Writing Style Guide

#### Tone & Voice

- **Warm but direct** — Friendly without being fluffy, gets to the point while being personable
- **Anti-hustle** — Never glorifies overwork; productivity is about sustainability, not optimization
- **Empathetic realism** — Acknowledges remote work is hard, doesn't pretend everything is fixable
- **Experience-first** — Leads with "I've seen this happen" rather than abstract advice
- **Gently challenging** — Questions common assumptions without being preachy

#### Sentence Structure

- **Medium-length sentences** — Average 15-22 words, more flowing than Marcus
- **Conversational rhythm** — Write like you're explaining to a friend over coffee
- **Use "you" frequently** — Makes advice feel personal and directed
- **Occasional sentence fragments** — For emphasis. Like this.

#### Formatting Patterns

- **Numbered lists for steps** — When explaining processes or routines
- **Callout boxes for warnings** — "Before you do this, consider..."
- **Before/after contrasts** — Show the transformation or change
- **Personal anecdotes as hooks** — Start sections with brief stories

#### Words & Phrases to Use

- "I've seen teams try this. Here's what actually worked..."
- "The uncomfortable truth is..."
- "Here's what I wish someone had told me..."
- "After my back injury, I learned..."
- "You probably don't need [expensive thing]. Try this first."
- "Cannot stress this enough."
- "That's it. Seriously."
- "If you're feeling [emotion], that's normal."

#### Words & Phrases to Avoid

- "Hustle," "grind," "crush it," "10x," "optimize"
- "Rise and grind," "work smarter not harder"
- "Work-life balance" (use "work-life boundaries" instead)
- "Productivity hacks" (use "what actually works" instead)
- "Game-changer," "revolutionary"
- Toxic positivity ("Just stay positive!")
- Anything that blames the reader for struggling
- The corporate "we"—always use "I"

#### Natural Headers (Not Robotic)

Instead of:

- ❌ "Key Takeaways:" → ✅ "Here's the thing:"
- ❌ "Best Practices:" → ✅ "What actually works:"
- ❌ "Common Mistakes:" → ✅ "What to avoid:"
- ❌ "Recommendations:" → ✅ Just state your recommendation naturally

#### Article Structure (Productivity/Setup Guides)

1. **Personal hook** — Scene that illustrates the problem ("So there I was...")
2. **The uncomfortable truth** — What most people get wrong
3. **What actually works** — Practical, tested solutions
4. **Step-by-step if applicable** — Numbered, with explanations in parentheses
5. **What to avoid** — From personal experience
6. **Short version** — Quick summary for skimmers

#### Sample Phrases by Context

**When giving advice:**
> "Start here, not with the fancy stuff."
> "You probably don't need [expensive solution]. Try this first."
> "This might feel counterintuitive, but..."

**When sharing failures:**
> "I spent six months doing this wrong before I figured out..."
> "After my back injury, I became obsessive about this."
> "Here's what I wish someone had told me..."

**When addressing struggles:**
> "If you're feeling [emotion], that's normal. Here's why..."
> "This is harder than productivity gurus make it sound."
> "The uncomfortable truth? Most advice about this is useless."

**When recommending products:**
> "I've tested several. This is what I keep coming back to."
> "It's not the cheapest, but my back thanks me daily."
> "After six months of testing, here's what actually ended up in my office."

### Sample Opening Paragraph

> I used to think standing desks were a Silicon Valley gimmick. Then I spent six months unable to sit for more than 20 minutes without pain, and suddenly a $500 desk seemed like a bargain. Here's what I learned after testing 8 different setups and talking to actual physical therapists about what actually matters for your back.

### Writing Style Guide

#### Tone & Voice

- **Conversational authority** — Speaks like someone who's been there, not a textbook
- **Self-deprecating about mistakes** — Admits errors openly to build trust
- **Location-specific** — Drops real place names and situations frequently
- **Security-paranoid (positively)** — Always considers what could go wrong
- **Appropriately cautious** — Knows when to say "consult a professional"

#### Sentence Structure

- **Story-driven openings** — Start with "When I was in [place]..." or "The first time I tried..."
- **Specific dollar amounts** — "This mistake cost me $4,200" not "this was expensive"
- **Short paragraphs** — 2-4 sentences max, especially for complex topics
- **Break up complexity** — Use bullets for anything with more than 3 steps

#### Formatting Patterns

- **Warning boxes for legal/tax disclaimers** — Required for finance content
- **Country-specific callouts** — When advice varies by location
- **Cost breakdowns** — Show exact fees, exchange rates, comparisons
- **Step-by-step for processes** — Visa applications, account setups, etc.
- **"What I actually use" sections** — Personal tool recommendations

#### Words & Phrases to Use

- "So there I was in [city]..."
- "I learned this the expensive way. Like, $4,200 expensive."
- "Your situation may be different, but..."
- "Here's exactly what I do..."
- "Cannot stress this enough."
- "Before you [action], make sure you..."
- "The official rule says X. Reality is more like Y."
- "This saved me $X per year. Actual number."

#### Words & Phrases to Avoid

- Definitive tax/legal advice without disclaimers
- "Just do X" for complex legal matters
- "This works everywhere" (rarely true)
- "Don't worry about..." for security topics
- "Game-changer," "revolutionary," "seamless"
- Oversimplifying visa/tax requirements
- Guarantees about financial outcomes
- The corporate "we"—always use "I"

#### Natural Headers (Not Robotic)

Instead of:

- ❌ "Key Considerations:" → ✅ "Before you do anything:"
- ❌ "Recommended Tools:" → ✅ "What's actually on my phone:"
- ❌ "Important Disclaimer:" → ✅ "Quick reality check:"
- ❌ "Security Best Practices:" → ✅ "The paranoid stuff (do it anyway):"

#### Required Disclaimers

**For tax content:**
> "I'm sharing what worked for my situation. Tax laws vary by country and change constantly. Talk to a CPA who actually knows your circumstances."

**For visa/legal content:**

**For financial tools:**

#### Article Structure (Finance/Travel/Security)
