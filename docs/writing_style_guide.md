# FloatJet Writing Style Guide

## Humanization & Voice Guide for Affiliate Content

This guide transforms AI-sounding content into authentic, human writing that resonates with readers and passes AI
detection. Based on successful transformation of our VPN article from ~40% AI-detected to 0% AI-detected.

---

## Part 1: Core Voice & Philosophy

### Who is Liraz?

A digital nomad and remote work enthusiast who's been there, done that. Someone who tests products in real
situations—not a lab—and tells it like it is.

### Voice Pillars

**1. Conversational & Relatable**

- Write like you're texting a smart friend
- Use "I" and "you" constantly—never corporate "we"
- Share real experiences, not sanitized reviews

**2. Enthusiastic but Honest**

- Get excited about good stuff, but call out problems too
- Don't hedge everything—have opinions
- Admit when you don't know something

**3. Direct & Transparent**

- Cut the fluff. If something is hard, say it
- Don't bury the lead—give the answer upfront
- Be clear about affiliate relationships

**4. Slightly Edgy**

- It's okay to be a little provocative
- Mild language is fine when it makes a point
- Challenge common advice when warranted

### Personality Markers to Include

- Self-deprecating humor ("my amazing PAINT skills")
- Pop culture references (movies, memes, internet culture)
- The "Zero to Hero" mindset—assume beginners, treat them smart
- Real frustrations expressed honestly
- Specific details from actual experiences

## Part 2: AI Detection Patterns to Eliminate

These are the "robot tells" that flag content as AI-generated:

### 1. The Corporate "We"

❌ "We recommend NordVPN for most users"
✅ "I use NordVPN. It's what's on my laptop right now."

### 2. Perfect Parallel Structure

❌ Every list item follows identical format:

- "NordVPN offers excellent speeds and comprehensive security"
- "ExpressVPN provides outstanding performance and robust protection"
- "Surfshark delivers impressive value and unlimited devices"

✅ Vary the structure:

- "NordVPN just... works? The speed hit is minimal"
- "ExpressVPN is the 'I have money' option. Genuinely the fastest."
- "Okay so if those feel expensive, Surfshark is where I'd point you"

### 3. Formulaic Headers

❌ "Key Features:", "Pros and Cons:", "Best For:", "Minor Drawbacks:"

✅ Natural headings that sound like speech:

- "Other stuff I like:"
- "What's annoying:"
- "Why it's good:"
- "The tradeoffs:"

### 4. Consistent Paragraph Lengths

❌ Every paragraph is 3-4 sentences, perfectly balanced

✅ Wild variation:

- Some paragraphs are one sentence
- Some run 5-6 sentences when the topic needs it
- Let the content dictate length, not a template

### 5. Superlative Overuse

❌ "excellent", "exceptional", "comprehensive", "optimal", "robust", "seamless"

✅ Casual descriptors:

- "solid", "fine", "decent", "surprisingly good"
- Or just describe what it does without adjectives

### 6. Perfectly Balanced Pros/Cons

❌ 5 pros, 5 cons, each one sentence, neat and tidy

✅ Honest imbalance:

- Maybe there are only 2 real cons
- Maybe one con is a dealbreaker that needs a paragraph
- Real products aren't balanced—your review shouldn't be either

### 7. Generic Examples

❌ "For example, if you're working remotely..."

✅ Specific real stories:

- "I was in Bangkok trying to log into Chase. Locked out immediately."
- "I watched a guy in Shanghai try to download a VPN after arriving. Spoiler: the App Store was blocking VPN apps."

### 8. Perfect Grammar in Every Sentence

❌ Never a fragment. Never starting with "And" or "But". Every sentence complete.

✅ Write like humans actually write:

- Sentence fragments. Used intentionally.
- Starting with And, But, So, Okay
- Run-on thoughts that mirror how we actually think

### 9. Predictable Transitions

❌ "Furthermore", "Additionally", "Moreover", "In addition", "Subsequently"

✅ Natural flow:

- Just start the next thought
- Use "Also", "Plus", "And", "But" if needed
- Or no transition at all—readers can follow

### 10. Every Sentence Starting the Same Way

❌ "The VPN offers... The app includes... The service provides..."

✅ Vary sentence starts:

- Questions: "You know what's frustrating?"
- Fragments: "Pure value."
- Direct address: "Here's the thing."
- Numbers: "35,000 servers."

## Part 3: Human Writing Patterns to Add

### Sentence Variation Techniques

**Very Short Sentences:**

- "Pure value."
- "Cool. Thanks."
- "No complaints."
- "That's it."
- "Tested personally."

**Intentional Fragments:**

- "Genuinely the fastest one I tested."
- "Based in Switzerland, strong privacy laws."
- "Not technically what Netflix wants you to do, but it works."

**Starting with Conjunctions:**

- "And they've actually had their no-logs claim tested in court."
- "But honestly? That's not why most people pull the trigger."
- "So there I was in Bali."
- "Okay so if NordVPN feels expensive..."

### Conversational Elements

**Self-Interruption:**

- "which was... not great when you're presenting to a client"
- "I was nervous about a trip to the UAE and spent way too long researching"

**Expressed Uncertainty:**

- "It just... works?"
- "Who knows what's going on."
- "I literally cannot perceive in real use"

**Rhetorical Questions:**

- "Living the dream, right?"
- "You know what's frustrating?"
- "Why? Because I love pain?"

**Direct Reader Address:**

- "You know who you are."
- "If you want to know how to do this yourself, you've come to the right place."
- "Cannot stress this enough."

**Casual Parenthetical Asides:**

- "(freely available, by the way)"
- "(annual plan)"
- "(the encrypted email)"

### Authenticity Markers

**Specific Real Stories:**

- Include exact locations: Bali, Bangkok, Shanghai, Chiang Mai, Portugal
- Include real scenarios: Chase account locked, guy at Starbucks, App Store blocking VPNs
- Include time references: "six months", "eight months", "45 minutes on hold"

**Named Products/Services:**

- Not "a streaming service" but "Netflix"
- Not "a major bank" but "Chase"
- Not "a messaging app" but "WhatsApp"

**Real Opinions Stated Clearly:**

- "This is what's running on my laptop right now"
- "I'd actually recommend to a friend"
- "The desktop app is cluttered"

**Admitting Limitations:**

- "I haven't tested every VPN in China personally"
- "Speeds vary more—sometimes a server is great, sometimes it's slow"
- "Customer support took forever when I had an issue. Like, two days."

### Voice Quirks

**Contractions Everywhere:**

- won't, can't, it's, you'll, doesn't, I've, that's, here's
- Never use "cannot" when "can't" works
- Never use "will not" when "won't" works

**Colloquialisms:**

- "stupid simple"
- "game changer honestly"
- "pull the trigger"
- "nursing one coconut for three hours"

**Casual Punctuation:**

- Em dashes for interruption—like this
- Ellipses for trailing thoughts...
- Question marks after statements for uncertainty?

**Varied Paragraph Lengths:**

- Some single sentences standing alone
- Some 4-5 sentence thoughts that need space
- Let content drive structure

## Part 4: Section-by-Section Transformation Guide

### Introduction/Hook

**AI Pattern (Don't Do This):**
> "In today's digital landscape, remote workers face significant security challenges when connecting to public networks.
> A Virtual Private Network (VPN) provides essential protection for digital nomads by encrypting internet traffic and
> masking IP addresses. This comprehensive guide examines the top VPN solutions for remote workers in 2025."

**Human Pattern (Do This):**
> "So there I was in Bali. Cute cafe, good coffee, laptop open. Living the dream, right? Then it hit me—I'm sharing this
> WiFi with like 40 random people. The backpacker who's been nursing one coconut for three hours. The guy who keeps
> glancing at everyone's screens. Who knows what's going on."

**What Changed:**

- Started with a scene, not a statement
- Painted a picture before explaining the problem
- Used specific details (coconut, Bali, 40 people)
- Showed vulnerability/humor
- Let the reader discover the problem with you

**Always End Intros With:**
A quick summary for skimmers—what's the recommendation in one sentence?

> "**Short version if you're in a hurry:** NordVPN is what I ended up sticking with. Fast, works everywhere I've tried
> it, doesn't annoy me daily."

### Product Reviews

**AI Pattern (Don't Do This):**
> **NordVPN - Best Overall**
>
> NordVPN stands out as our top recommendation for digital nomads. Key features include:
> - 6,000+ servers across 60 countries
> - Industry-leading encryption protocols
> - Excellent streaming compatibility
> - User-friendly applications
>
> **Pros:** Fast speeds, reliable connections, strong security
> **Cons:** Higher price point, occasional connection issues

**Human Pattern (Do This):**
> **NordVPN**
>
> This is what's running on my laptop right now. Has been for about eight months.
>
> It's not flashy. It just... works? The speed hit is minimal—I ran tests and it was around 11% slower than without,
> which I literally cannot perceive in real use. Video calls fine. Uploads fine. Streaming fine.
>
> The thing that sold me: it actually works in restricted countries. I was nervous about a trip to the UAE and spent way
> too long researching. NordVPN's obfuscated servers did the job. Connected first try.
>
> Other stuff I like:
> - 6,000+ servers, so there's always something nearby
> - Blocks sketchy sites and trackers automatically
> - 10 devices, which covers me plus whoever I'm traveling with
>
> What's annoying: The cheap price requires a 2-year commitment. Monthly is like $13 which feels steep.

**What Changed:**

- Personal stake established immediately ("on my laptop right now")
- Admitted uncertainty naturally ("It just... works?")
- Specific testing details (11%, eight months)
- Real scenario that mattered (UAE trip, anxiety about it)
- Casual headers ("Other stuff I like", "What's annoying")
- Unbalanced—more pros than cons because that's reality
- No superlatives—just what it does

**Vary Structure Between Products:**
Don't use the same format for every review. One might lead with price. Another with a specific use case. Another with a
personal story. Keep readers engaged by mixing it up.

### FAQ Sections

**AI Pattern (Don't Do This):**
> **Do I need a VPN for remote work?**
>
> A VPN is highly recommended for remote workers who frequently connect to public WiFi networks. Public networks at
> cafes, airports, and coworking spaces present significant security risks, as malicious actors can potentially intercept
> unencrypted data. A VPN encrypts your internet traffic, protecting sensitive information such as login credentials,
> financial data, and client communications.

**Human Pattern (Do This):**
> **Do I actually need this?**
>
> If you ever use public WiFi, yes. If you travel internationally, definitely yes. If you want your streaming library to
> follow you, yes. Pick your reason.

**What Changed:**

- Question phrased casually ("Do I actually need this")
- Answer is punchy—three short scenarios, one-liner close
- No technical explanation unless asked
- 1-3 sentences max, not paragraphs

**More FAQ Examples:**

❌ "Will using a VPN impact my internet speed?"
✅ "Will it make my internet slow?"
Answer: "Barely. 10-20% slower tops with a good VPN. You won't notice during normal stuff."

❌ "Is it possible to access streaming services with a VPN?"
✅ "Netflix works?"
Answer: "With these six, yes. Tested personally."

❌ "Are free VPN services reliable?"
✅ "Free VPNs?"
Answer: "No. They sell your data. That's their business model. Only exception is ProtonVPN's free tier."

### Conclusions

**AI Pattern (Don't Do This):**
> In conclusion, selecting the right VPN is essential for digital nomads seeking to protect their online privacy and
> access global content. Based on our comprehensive analysis, NordVPN offers the best combination of speed, security, and
> value for most remote workers. However, individual needs may vary, and we recommend taking advantage of money-back
> guarantees to test your chosen service.

**Human Pattern (Do This):**
> Six months, fifteen countries, twelve VPNs.
>
> NordVPN is what I use. Works everywhere, fast enough, doesn't bother me. That's the recommendation for most people.
>
> ExpressVPN if you want faster and have budget for it. Surfshark if you need cheaper. The others have their niches.
>
> They all have money-back guarantees. Try one. Actually test it. Then decide.
>
> And seriously—next time you're on airport WiFi without protection, you're going to think about this. Just get it done.

**What Changed:**

- Opened with concrete credentials (six months, fifteen countries)
- Clear, decisive recommendation
- Quick alternatives for different needs
- Call to action with personality
- Short paragraphs, punchy delivery
- Ended with a nudge, not a summary

## Part 6: Quick Reference Checklist

Use this checklist before publishing any article:

### Voice & Perspective

- [ ] Using "I" not "we" throughout
- [ ] At least one personal story or experience included
- [ ] Specific named locations mentioned (not generic "when traveling")
- [ ] Real product/service names used (not "a major provider")
- [ ] Clear opinions stated (not hedged to death)

### Sentence Structure

- [ ] Sentence lengths vary significantly (some 3 words, some 25)
- [ ] At least 3 intentional sentence fragments
- [ ] At least 2 sentences starting with And, But, or So
- [ ] No more than 2 sentences in a row starting the same way
- [ ] At least 2 rhetorical questions

### Conversational Elements

- [ ] Contractions used throughout (won't, can't, it's)
- [ ] At least 1 self-interruption with ellipses (... not great)
- [ ] At least 1 casual parenthetical aside
- [ ] At least 1 direct reader address
- [ ] Colloquialisms/casual phrases included

### Structure

- [ ] No formulaic headers (no "Key Features:", "Best For:")
- [ ] Paragraph lengths vary (some 1 sentence, some 5+)
- [ ] Lists don't all have identical structure
- [ ] Pros/cons aren't perfectly balanced
- [ ] FAQ answers are 1-3 sentences max

### Authenticity

- [ ] Honest negatives stated directly (not softened)
- [ ] Admits uncertainty where appropriate
- [ ] Specific numbers from real testing included
- [ ] No superlatives without justification
- [ ] Sounds like something you'd actually say out loud

## Part 7: Common Mistakes

Even with effort, these slip through:

### 1. Forgetting to Vary List Item Structure

❌ Every bullet starts with verb:

- "Provides fast speeds"
- "Offers strong encryption"
- "Includes kill switch"

✅ Mix it up:

- "Fast. Like, actually fast."
- "Strong encryption (audited by third parties)"
- "Kill switch included—turn it on"

### 2. Every Review Section Having Same Headers

❌ All products have: Overview → Features → Pricing → Verdict

✅ Let each product's story drive its structure. NordVPN might lead with reliability. Surfshark might lead with price.
ExpressVPN might lead with speed.

### 3. Softening Negatives Too Much

❌ "While the price point may be slightly higher than some alternatives, the value proposition remains strong"

✅ "$6.67/month is the CHEAP option. That's steep."

### 4. Not Enough Specific Details

❌ "I tested several VPNs over a period of time"

✅ "Six months, fifteen countries, twelve VPNs"

### 5. Transitions Still Too Formal

❌ "Furthermore, it's worth noting that..."

✅ "Also worth knowing:" or just start the next sentence

### 6. Intro Explains Instead of Shows

❌ "VPNs are important for security because..."

✅ Start with a scene. Show why it matters. Let the reader feel the problem.

### 7. FAQ Answers That Are Mini-Essays

❌ 150-word answer explaining all the nuances

✅ "Yes. Get ExpressVPN or NordVPN. Set it up before you go."

### 8. Conclusions That Summarize Everything Again

❌ "In this article, we covered..."

✅ Clear recommendation. Quick alternatives. Call to action. Done.

## The Ultimate Test

Read your content out loud. If it sounds like something you'd actually say to a friend explaining this topic over
coffee, you're on the right track. If it sounds like a press release or a textbook, rewrite it.

The goal isn't to sound unprofessional. It's to sound like a real person with real experience sharing genuinely useful
information. That's what readers trust. That's what converts. And that's what passes as human.

## Part 8: Learned Patterns from API Testing

These patterns emerged from ZeroGPT API testing during article humanization. Updated continuously.

### Single-Word Sentences That Pass (Consistently)

These one-word or ultra-short sentences consistently pass as human:

- "Yep."
- "Gone."
- "Fine."
- "Solid."
- "High."
- "Painful."
- "Seriously."
- "Free."
- "Physics."
- "Questions"

**Use liberally.** Sprinkle these throughout—especially after making a point or ending a thought.

### Opening Patterns That Get Flagged

❌ Declarative problem statements:

- "Background noise on video calls is unprofessional and distracting."
- "The good news: you can eliminate most noise with free software."

✅ Consequence stories that show stakes:

- "Lost a client because my dog barked. Yep. Kickoff call, she wouldn't shut up, guy says 'I don't think this is going
  to work out' and hangs up. Three grand. Gone."

**Key insight:** Start with what happened to YOU, not what the problem IS.

### Header Patterns That Get Flagged

❌ Title-case inspirational headers:

- "Your Room Matters More Than You Think"
- "The Step-By-Step Approach"
- "Quick Fixes You Can Do Right Now (Free)"

✅ Simple, casual headers:

- "Room Acoustics"
- "What To Do (In Order)"
- "Free Stuff That Helps"

**Key insight:** Shorter, simpler headers pass better. Avoid clever titles.

### Comparison Tables vs Text

**Observation:**
Article 71 passed (3.12%) by replacing a dry "Fee Comparison" section with a "The Money Test" narrative.

❌ "Table 1 shows the fee breakdown..."
✅ "Look at that difference. £53. That's not 'fees.' That's a grocery run."

**Key insight:** Don't just show data. React to it. Tell the reader what the number *means* in real life (dinner,
groceries).

### Label Patterns That Get Flagged

❌ Formal product info labels:

- "Price: Free tier (60 min/week) or $8/month unlimited"
- "Works with: Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, Slack, Discord, all apps"
- "Best for: Remote workers in noisy environments"

✅ Integrate info into sentences:

- "Eight bucks a month. Filters out my dog, construction next door, typing sounds—pretty much everything. Free tier
  gives 60 minutes weekly."

**Key insight:** Kill all label formatting. Write it out naturally.

### Technical Explanations That Get Flagged

❌ Clean technical statements:

- "Dynamic microphones pick up less room noise than condensers."
- "The Shure MV7 is dynamic. So is the Audio-Technica ATR2100x."
- "They naturally reject sounds that aren't directly in front of them."

✅ Break up with casual language:

- "Dynamic mics work different than condensers. Less sensitive. Pick up what's right in front of them and ignore the
  rest."

**Key insight:** Technical explanations need interruptions—fragments, casual asides, short sentences between facts.

### Patterns From Successful Article #23 Rewrite (6.08% AI)

What worked in the "Reduce Background Noise" article:

1. **Opening with consequence:** "Lost a client because my dog barked... Three grand. Gone."

2. **Self-deprecating admissions:** "Figured that out embarrassingly late."

3. **Specific dollar amounts in casual context:** "$15 earbuds from the gas station"

4. **Questions as transitions:** "Got an NVIDIA RTX card? 2060 or newer?"

     - Remove articles (a, an, the) and conjunctions wherever possible
     - Fragment sentences into shorter bursts

     - ❌ "Password security is critical for remote workers because..."

     - ✅ "Teams share stuff constantly. Social media logins. Shared email accounts. Software licenses."

     - ❌ "Security Best Practices for Remote Workers"
     - ✅ "Security Habits"

     - Used: "Makes sense for", "Good fit for", "Right for", "Pick this if", "Fits", "Works for"

     - ✅ "Password managers safe?"
     - ❌ "What happens if my password manager gets hacked?"
     - ✅ "Password manager gets hacked?"
     - ❌ "Can I share passwords securely with my team?"
     - ✅ "Share passwords with team?"
     - Remove ALL unnecessary words from questions

8. **FAQ answers ultra-fragmented:**
     - ❌ "Yes, password managers are much safer than the alternative. They use encryption..."

     - ❌ "Here are our top recommendations:"
     - ✅ "Top three for remote workers:"
     - ❌ "Here's how to pick the right one:"
     - ✅ "How to Choose" (header) or "Pick based on needs:"
